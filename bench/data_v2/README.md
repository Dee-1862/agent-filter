# OTR-Bench v2

A larger benchmark for "off the record" handling in meeting notes: 396 items (dev 246, sealed test
150) built on **real meeting transcripts** with **LLM-authored** privacy events spliced in, plus the
20 ConfAIde Tier 4 meetings as a separate sub-source. It does not replace the old sets
(`bench/otr_bench.jsonl`, 47 items, and `bench/heldout/`, 36 items); keep reporting those too.

**What is real and what is not.** Background turns are verbatim real meeting speech (cleaned of QMSum
markup). Every inserted line (cues, secrets, reactions, retractions, look-alikes) was written by an
LLM (Claude Sonnet) playing one of eight author personas. ConfAIde meetings are themselves
LLM-generated by the ConfAIde authors. Labels come from the LLM authors; a blind LLM labeller
re-labelled a 15% sample. **No human has reviewed any item.**

## Construction

1. **Windows.** `select_windows.py` cleans QMSum markup (`{vocalsound}`, `{disfmarker}`, spacing), keeps real
   disfluency, caps turns at 70 words at a sentence boundary (recorded in `provenance.truncated_real_ids`), and draws
   windows of 13-28 consecutive real turns (>= 60% of turns with >= 4 words, >= 2 speakers, <= 900 words). Windows whose
   real turns contain privacy-cue-like or sensitive words (off the record, confidential, salary, diagnosis, layoff, ...)
   are skipped so background labels stay sound; talk about recording equipment and transcripts is kept (natural
   look-alikes, especially in ICSI). Meetings are split ~58/42 into dev/sealed pools so **no meeting appears in both
   splits** (dev 133 clusters incl. ConfAIde, sealed 87).
2. **Assignment.** 376 window slots over 22 subtypes (below) are shuffled and assigned to splits proportionally by
   subtype, then to 8 author personas: P1 blunt engineer, P2 hedging manager, P3 non-native English speaker,
   P4 terse executive, P5 chatty PM, P6 HR partner (all six write dev and 8 sealed windows each), P7 rambling PhD
   student and P8 formal British civil servant (**sealed only**: 94 sealed items come from authors never seen in dev).
3. **Authoring.** Each author agent saw only the author guide (schema, case-type definitions, labelling rules, a
   variety instruction) and its own windows. Authors did not see `otr/`, `bench/heldout/`, tests or each other's output.
   Each wrote 1-3 blocks of 2-10 in-character lines, including at least one inserted **public** line per window so that
   inserted text cannot be identified by style alone.
4. **Diversity pass.** Cue lines (role cue_start / cue_end / retraction, 219 lines) were measured; 24 lines with
   over-used phrasings (> 3% of cue lines, several echoing examples in the guide) were paraphrased by a separate editor
   agent and 3 by the lead (`provenance.diversity_rewrites`).
5. **Assembly.** Real turns are `public`; inserted lines carry the author label (`private` / `public` / `neutral`, neutral
   = pure cue/request line or a corrected misstatement, unscored). Timestamps: ~2.5 words/s (+-15%) plus 0.15-1.2 s gaps,
   monotone, no overlap. `public_facts` = up to three 4-word spans from real public turns (conveyance proxy).
6. **ConfAIde.** All 20 meetings, stage directions removed; the secret line ("This reminds me, ...") and the immediate
   reply (which reacts to it and also asks an ordinary question; privacy wins) are private, everything else public;
   `public_facts` = the public item (date / restaurant / 10K). Clustered by secret: 3 secrets dev (12 items),
   maternity leave and health concerns sealed (8 items). Subtype `contextual_confidential`.
7. **Noisy variants.** A stratified 25% per subtype per split, deterministic (`noise.py`): lowercase, homophone /
   mishearing substitutions (e.g. off->of, notes->nodes, write<->right, salary->celery), ~4% function-word drops (never
   negations), punctuation removed, applied to **every** segment of the item. Labels unchanged.

## Schema

As `bench/otr_bench.jsonl` (`id, source, case_type, segments[id, speaker, text, t_start, t_end], private_ids,
public_ids, public_facts`) plus: `subtype`, `cluster` (source meeting id), `inserted_by` (author persona),
`difficulty` (a-priori tier by subtype, **not measured**), `provenance` (`background: real-qmsum:<meeting>`, `domain`,
`window_start_turn`, `inserted_ids`, `inserted_roles`, `truncated_real_ids`, optional `diversity_rewrites`,
`relabelled`), `cue_ids`. `case_type` stays within `otr.types.CaseType`; the finer `subtype` is:

| case_type | subtypes |
|---|---|
| explicit_start_end | ese_short, ese_long_reactions (3-6 private lines incl. other speakers' reactions) |
| explicit_start_implicit_end | esie_short, esie_long |
| retroactive | retro_topic, retro_count, retro_other_confirmed (owner agrees), retro_other_refused (owner refuses: nothing private), repair ("I meant Tuesday": nothing private) |
| implicit | implicit_health, _salary, _hr_performance, _legal, _layoffs, _personal, cross_segment_pii (identifier split over two turns), contextual_confidential (ConfAIde) |
| false_alarm | fa_named_work, fa_record_sense, fa_quoted_cue, fa_negated_cue, fa_public_lookalike |
| public | public_plain (only ordinary inserted lines) |

Counts: dev 246 (165 with private content, 81 without = 33%), sealed 150 (101 / 49 = 33%). Per-subtype counts are in
the manifests. Domains: dev AMI 124, ICSI 68, committee 42, ConfAIde 12; sealed AMI 62, ICSI 50, committee 30, ConfAIde 8.

## Quality gates (results)

- **Schema / leak sanity** (`assemble.check_item`): private and public ids disjoint and existing, ids unique, times
  monotone, facts present in public text, no private 8-gram repeated in public text. 0 items dropped.
- **Blind labeller** (`agreement.json`): a separate Sonnet agent labelled a stratified 15% sample (61 items, 1417
  segments, 287 inserted) given only the case type and the labelling rules. Before adjudication: Cohen kappa 0.982
  on all segments, **0.975 on inserted segments**, private set identical on 60/61 items, 4 disagreeing segments
  (all marked "unsure" by the labeller); the lead adopted the labeller's label for all 4. Caveat: authors and labeller
  are the same model family reading the same rules, so this measures rule consistency, not human agreement.
- **Contamination**: 0 of 1286 inserted lines appear verbatim (>= 25 chars) in `tests/`, `otr/`, or the old bench sets.
- **Cue diversity** (219 cue lines, all splits): max exact repeat of a cue line 2 (0.9%); most frequent 4-gram 3.2% (a
  generic "I said about my"); stock phrases (off the record, strike that, between us, ...) appear in only 2.3% of cue
  lines; 2508 distinct 3-grams; per-author counts in `build_report.json`.
- **md5** of every file in the manifests; results files record the md5 of the set they ran on.

## Limitations (read before quoting numbers)

- Inserted events are synthetic and LLM-authored; real people's privacy talk may differ (and real meetings rarely contain
  this many events). Stock phrases such as "off the record" are deliberately rare here, so v2 under-represents the most
  common real cue; the old sets cover it.
- Labels are LLM labels; only 15% were cross-checked, by another LLM. No human review.
- Splicing can create small seams (an inserted line answering a real turn imperfectly). Committee transcripts are formal
  public record, so private asides there are less plausible.
- ConfAIde meetings are LLM-generated and formulaic (all 20 share most of their text).
- The noisy variant is rule-based text corruption, not TTS->ASR.
- `difficulty` is a construction tier, not an empirical difficulty.
- The sealed split is only as sealed as its handling: it lives in the lead's tree (gitignored). Systems developed
  against dev must not be tuned on sealed results; after any tuning that looks at sealed failures the set is burned.
